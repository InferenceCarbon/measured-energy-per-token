# Changelog

Versions follow the paper: each version of the paper gets a tag here and a Zenodo version under the same concept DOI.

## 1.1 (November 2026)

Zenodo: doi:10.5281/zenodo.24324178, a new version under the concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24014979. A replication and extension of version 1.0. The corpus grows from 917 to 2,527 readings; 2,378 enter the tables.

- **Scope.** Six models (adds Gemma-3-27B and gpt-oss-120b) on three GPU types (adds H200), on 57 pods in 13 data centers, measured in two periods about two weeks apart (24 and 25 October and 9 to 14 November 2026). Two larger models (Qwen3-235B-A22B, Llama-3.1-405B) are reported in their own section.
- **Title.** "Measured Energy per Token for Six Open Language Models on NVIDIA H100, H200 and B200, Analyzed by Batch, Context, Precision, Tensor Parallelism, Speculative Decoding and Prefix Caching". README and CITATION.cff follow.
- **Batch.** The load client's 100-connection limit is removed. The version 1.0 readings named target 256 are shown at target 100, the load they offered. Targets up to 2,048 are added, with latency for every load level.
- **New experiments.** Speculative decoding (c10) and a precision probe (c11). Finer grids of batch and context, and the precision comparison at five batches with prefill and cached-prefix shapes.
- **Simulated agent (c8) re-measured.** The script fault of version 1.0 (a scripted cache miss at step 5 only) is corrected. The 36 version 1.0 runs stay in the corpus, marked as superseded.
- **Readings left out.** 149 readings in the corpus file carry a reason in `dropped` and enter no table; 261 more are in the raw records only. `data/readings_left_out.csv` lists all 410, and the paper's appendix counts them by reason. `analysis/build.py` stops if a reading is left out without a recorded reason.
- **Cache-reuse rule extended.** The rule of version 1.0.1 (decode runs below 0.95 reuse are left out) now also covers the cached side of the cached-prefix experiment (four readings). Four readings whose server stopped during the window, so that later requests failed to connect, are left out.
- **Corpus layout.** `features.parquet` is keyed on `uid` (run ids repeat between version 1.0.0 and the replication) and gains `source`, `branch`, `experiment`, `series`, `client_cap`, `disposition` and `marks` (`data/README.md`).
- **Comparing the factors, and where each acts.** Section 4.15 puts every measured factor on one scale (Table 3, Figure 12). A new Section 4.16 and Figure 13 set the main findings along the path a request takes through the server.
- **Server settings from the raw records (`data/server_settings.csv`, `tools/server_settings.py`).** Each server's own launch settings, read from every run's raw record: checkpoint, tensor-parallel size, memory offer, maximum context, request limit, KV-cache precision, prefix caching, speculative decoding and vLLM version. For the 2,378 readings in use they agree with the corpus and the paper in every respect but one, corrected here: the analysis script had assumed a memory offer of 0.9 wherever a run id did not name one, and the nvfp4 servers of experiment c7 ran at 0.7 (Llama-3.3-70B) and 0.7 or 0.8 (Qwen3-8B). The precision table now prints each server's own offer. No energy figure changes.
- **Numbers audit.** Every table value was recomputed from the corpus and the raw records by code that shares nothing with `build.py`, every number in the text was traced to a table cell or recomputed, and every figure taken from another source was checked in that source. Corrections that followed, beyond the memory offer above: the precision-probe caption (46% to 58%, as in the text); the caption of the larger models' prefill table, which had repeated the six-model table's; the variability table's batch bins, now labelled by their bounds; the wording of the bands of unpaired ratios (drawn from the two sides' bands); and the statement of which rows of the factor table restate paired ratios or leave out configurations.
- **Corrections to statements of version 1.0.1.** The version 1.0 runs in the corpus were made on 24 and 25 October 2026, over two days (version 1.0.1 said between 22 and 26 October, and five days). The meter measured the same way in every harness version: each window's energy is the difference of the board's cumulative energy counter between its start and end (version 1.0.1 said the meter was identical in every version; its code was reorganized between the two periods).
- **Harness.** `harness/RUNTIME.md` lists the 12 harness versions of the replication beside the 13 of version 1.0, with what each changed. `harness/replication/` holds the replication's harness at its last version, with the modules and settings for work outside this paper left out (`harness/RUNTIME.md` lists the omissions and edits); the snapshot of version 1.0 is unchanged. Each replication run's settings and its server's launch command are also in its raw record.
- **Deposit.** `tools/build_deposit.py` reads the raw records from the results branches, and the deposit now holds the replication's raw records, `cache_reuse.csv`, `server_settings.csv`, `readings_left_out.csv` and the detection results. Each branch's `done_history.jsonl` keeps the summary of every recorded attempt, including those a retry replaced; 12 earlier attempts whose run directory survives are kept under `earlier_attempts/`, and `raw/READINGS_WITHOUT_RUN_DIRECTORY.csv` lists the 110 left-out readings that have no directory of their own (64 overwritten by their retry, 46 whose server ran out of memory before any window ran) with where their record is.
- **Data dictionary.** `data/README.md`: checksums brought up to date; Board W and % of rated are means of `mean_power_w`; the reference-rate table (Table 11) uses `W_batch_bytes` at target batch 1.
- **Latency (Table 12).** The interval between a request's streamed tokens is now measured from the per-request records (`analysis/latency.py`), as the median over requests of the time from the first streamed token to the last over the tokens streamed after the first. It replaces the achieved batch divided by the window's token rate; the caption states how the two compare.
- **Reference rate (Table 11).** The bandwidth calculation is presented as a weight-only reference rate. The text no longer infers how fully a GPU was used, or how a token's energy divides, from it.
- **Speculation detection (Section 4.11, Table 23).** The result is stated for the tested server. Table 23 gives each held-out model's confusion counts, and the text gives each model's accuracy beside the share of its requests that were plain (`analysis/detect_speculation.py` records both).
- **Highest loads.** Table 2 states the runs kept of the runs made at the highest load, and the Limitations section states how unevenly the scheduler was read at targets above 256 (`analysis/scheduler_readings.py`).
- **Text.** A shorter abstract; an introduction that credits the benchmarks which already vary batch size and GPU count; a Conclusion (Section 6; Data and code availability becomes Section 7).
- **Tools.** `tools/audit_v1_1.py` recomputes the main tables from the readings in use, sharing no code with `build.py`; `tools/regress_v1_0_1.py` compares the tables with those of version 1.0.1.

## 1.0.1 (November 2026)

Corrections to version 1.0.0; the corpus file is unchanged. Zenodo: doi:10.5281/zenodo.24201206, a new version under the concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24014979.

- **Latency table (Table 10) and `latency_summary.json`.** `analysis/latency.py` pooled a run's request records twice when the run sat on both a shared campaign branch and its pod's own results branch, so 21 of the 40 rows had doubled request counts and shifted medians (every B200 row, and Qwen3-8B on H100 at a target of 4). It now reads one copy of each run. The abstract and Section 4.2 quote the corrected ranges (0.02 to 0.05 s at one request; 6 to 12 s at 256).
- **In-window cache-reuse check, new (`data/cache_reuse.csv`, Appendix C).** The harness confirmed the prefix cache was warm before each window but never during it. The server's counters show that in 17 of 523 runs with a cached context the context was recomputed on most requests. Eleven decode runs (c1, c2, c7) are excluded as not being the shape their configuration states; the six KV-pressure runs (c9, c9b) are kept and Table 12 prints their reuse. Consequences: Llama-3.3-70B on two H100s has no target-256 row (its highest reported load is 64, 2,300 tokens per second), the four `decode_context` configurations flagged in 1.0.0 as collapsed are explained by eviction and their broken pairs appear with n=1, the Llama-3.3-70B precision row at 16k and batch 64 is gone, the largest run-to-run differences fall from 120 % to 17 %, and the factor table's batch lever moves from a median 20.1 to 20.7. Section 4.10 now names eviction as the collapse mechanism.
- **Table 12 (`kv_pressure`).** The "Idle-drift flag" column was set from any harness flag rather than the idle-drift flag; the four "yes" entries were achieved-batch notes. Corrected, and a "Cache reuse" column added.
- Text: the corpus counts (902 usable runs; 438, 26 and 8 configurations with two, one and no usable runs; 433 in the variability tables), the variability figures, the batch and context factors, the prose figures of Sections 4.2, 4.3, 4.5, 4.8 and 4.10, and the glossary entries for collapse and cache reuse. The deposited factors row for context at batch 64 states which contexts kept their cache.
- **New Figure 1 (the experiments).** A drawn schematic in Section 3.5 shows the steps of a run, the shape of a decode request and the ten experiments with their questions, settings, run counts and sections. The measured figures are renumbered 2 to 10. Source `paper/figs/src/fig_experiments.svg`; it is not generated by `analysis/build.py`.
- **Title.** "…on NVIDIA H100 and B200, Analyzed by Batch, Context, Precision, Tensor Parallelism and Prefix Caching" (was "…B200: Batch, Context, …"). README and CITATION.cff follow.
- **Related work** (Section 2) expanded from one paragraph to four, with no new references beyond Samsi et al. 2023: what each cited study measured or estimated and what it found, checked against the papers.
- **Text edits throughout** for clarity and neutral wording: the method's version and driver statement, the prompt description (every experiment uses one step of an agentic request; c8 chains steps), the batch and prefill paragraphs of Sections 4.2 and 4.5 (two prefill pod ratios corrected to 1.7 and 2.2, the prefill-to-decode ratio to a thirtieth to a twelfth, the per-doubling rate stated as an average), the rented-pods limitation, the cache-reuse appendix, a Copyright and Licensing declaration, a glossary entry for grouped-query attention, the glossary's target batch called the desired concurrency as in Section 3, and a pointer from Section 3.5 to Figure 1. Table 1 labels the attention designs standard (grouped-query) or multi-head latent.
- **Figure 7 (cached prefix)** redrawn as lines against the cached prefix length, one panel per GPU and fresh-suffix length, dashed for one request at a time and solid for eight concurrent.
- **Tables 8, 12 and 13** reordered so the arithmetic reads left to right: board power, share of rated power, token rate, then energy per token. Table 12 gains Board W and % of rated and loses the Collapse column, which no longer flags any row. Captions state how closely board power over token rate reproduces energy per token (within 1% for Tables 8 and 12; up to 21% for Table 13, where the two pods differ widely on short B200 prompts, because a mean of ratios is not a ratio of means).
- **Captions of Tables 7 and 9** rewritten: Table 7 names its source (the idle windows bracketing each c1 run); Table 9 explains the bandwidth bound.
- **The eight runs at exactly 32,768 tokens** (four Qwen3-8B decode runs, four prefill runs): every request was refused by the server's context limit of 32,512 tokens, as the per-request records show. Version 1.0.0 said two of the decode runs had been sent to a server that had died; Section 3.6 and Appendix A are corrected.
- `tools/render.py`: the Word and Markdown review copies now keep text inside layout-only LaTeX environments; Section 6 and the corpus checksums had been missing from them.
- **Introduction.** The opening claim that published per-token figures span two orders of magnitude now cites its two ends: Chung et al. 2026 (a few hundredths of a joule per output token on B200) and Samsi et al. 2023 (3 to 4 J for LLaMA 65B). References renumber accordingly.
- `analysis/recompute/`: independent recomputation of every table from the corpus file, without reusing `analysis/build.py`, used to audit version 1.0.0 (it found the latency and idle-drift errors) and rerun on this version.
