# Changelog

Versions follow the paper: each version of the paper gets a tag here and a Zenodo version under the same concept DOI.

## 1.0.1 (November 2026)

Corrections to version 1.0.0; the corpus file is unchanged. Zenodo: doi:10.5281/zenodo.24201206, a new version under the concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24014979.

- **Latency table (Table 10) and `latency_summary.json`.** `analysis/latency.py` pooled a run's request records twice when the run sat on both a shared campaign branch and its pod's own results branch, so 21 of the 40 rows had doubled request counts and shifted medians (every B200 row, and Qwen3-8B on H100 at a target of 4). It now reads one copy of each run. The abstract and Section 4.2 quote the corrected ranges (0.02 to 0.05 s at one request; 6 to 12 s at 256).
- **In-window cache-reuse check, new (`data/cache_reuse.csv`, Appendix C).** The harness confirmed the prefix cache was warm before each window but never during it. The server's counters show that in 17 of 523 runs with a cached context the context was recomputed on most requests. Eleven decode runs (c1, c2, c7) are excluded as not being the shape their configuration states; the six KV-pressure runs (c9, c9b) are kept and Table 12 prints their reuse. Consequences: Llama-3.3-70B on two H100s has no target-256 row (its highest reported load is 64, 2,300 tokens per second), the four `decode_context` configurations flagged in 1.0.0 as collapsed are explained by eviction and their broken pairs appear with n=1, the Llama-3.3-70B precision row at 16k and batch 64 is gone, the largest run-to-run differences fall from 120 % to 17 %, and the factor table's batch lever moves from a median 20.1 to 20.7. Section 4.10 now names eviction as the collapse mechanism.
- **Table 12 (`kv_pressure`).** The "Idle-drift flag" column was set from any harness flag rather than the idle-drift flag; the four "yes" entries were achieved-batch notes. Corrected, and a "Cache reuse" column added.
- Text: the corpus counts (902 usable runs; 438, 26 and 8 configurations with two, one and no usable runs; 433 in the variability tables), the variability figures, the batch and context factors, the prose figures of Sections 4.2, 4.3, 4.5, 4.8 and 4.10, and the glossary entries for collapse and cache reuse. The deposited factors row for context at batch 64 states which contexts kept their cache.
- **New Figure 1 (the experiments).** A drawn schematic in Section 3.5 shows the steps of a run, the shape of a decode request and the ten experiments with their questions, settings, run counts and sections. The measured figures are renumbered 2 to 10. Source `paper/figs/src/fig_experiments.svg`; it is not generated by `analysis/build.py`.
- **Title.** "…on NVIDIA H100 and B200, Analyzed by Batch, Context, Precision, Tensor Parallelism and Prefix Caching" (was "…B200: Batch, Context, …"). README and CITATION.cff follow.
- **Related work** (Section 2) expanded from one paragraph to four, with no new references beyond Samsi et al. 2023: what each cited study measured or estimated and what it found, checked against the papers.
- **Text edits throughout** for clarity and neutral wording: the method's version and driver statement, the prompt description (every experiment uses one step of an agentic request; c8 chains steps), the batch and prefill paragraphs of Sections 4.2 and 4.5 (two prefill pod ratios corrected to 1.7 and 2.2, the prefill-to-decode ratio to a thirtieth to a twelfth, the per-doubling rate stated as an average), the rented-pods limitation, the cache-reuse appendix, a Copyright and Licensing declaration, a glossary entry for grouped-query attention, the glossary's target batch called the desired concurrency as in Section 3, and a pointer from Section 3.5 to Figure 1. Table 1 labels the attention designs standard (grouped-query) or multi-head latent.
- **Figure 7 (cached prefix)** redrawn as lines against the cached prefix length, one panel per GPU and fresh-suffix length, dashed for one request at a time and solid for eight concurrent.
- **Tables 8, 12 and 13** reordered so the arithmetic reads left to right: board power, share of rated power, token rate, then energy per token. Table 12 gains Board W and % of rated and loses the Collapse column, which no longer flags any row. Captions state how closely board power over token rate reproduces energy per token (within 1% for Tables 8 and 12; up to 21% for Table 13, where the two pods differ widely on short B200 prompts, because a mean of ratios is not a ratio of means).
- **Captions of Tables 7 and 9** rewritten: Table 7 names its source (the idle windows bracketing each c1 run); Table 9 explains the bandwidth bound.
- **The eight runs at exactly 32,768 tokens** (four Qwen3-8B decode runs, four prefill runs): every request was refused by the server's context limit of 32,512 tokens, as the per-request records show. Version 1.0.0 said two of the decode runs had been sent to a server that had died; Section 3.6 and Appendix A are corrected.
- `tools/render.py`: the Word and Markdown review copies now keep text inside layout-only LaTeX environments; Section 6 and the corpus checksums had been missing from them.
- **Introduction.** The opening claim that published per-token figures span two orders of magnitude now cites its two ends: Chung et al. 2026 (a few hundredths of a joule per output token on B200) and Samsi et al. 2023 (3 to 4 J for LLaMA 65B). References renumber accordingly.
- `analysis/recompute/`: independent recomputation of every table from the corpus file, without reusing `analysis/build.py`, used to audit version 1.0.0 (it found the latency and idle-drift errors) and rerun on this version.
